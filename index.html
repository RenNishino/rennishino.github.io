life

A personal, single-user life-management tool. Not a product, not distributed.

What it does

life is a set of small scripts that run on my own Mac. Every morning at 07:03 a scheduled job assembles a single Markdown file — a briefing — and saves it to a folder on that machine. The briefing lists today's calendar events, the subject lines of unread email from the last two days, my own task list, and a count of how many days in the last week I recorded my sleep and wrote a diary entry.

That is the entire scope. There is no server, no website that displays my data, no account to sign up for, and no other user. I am the only person who uses it.

How it uses Google data

  • Google Calendar — reads events to list today's schedule and to total how much time I spent on each of my priorities, identified by event colour.
  • Gmail — reads message metadata only (sender, subject, date, labels) using the gmail.metadata scope. Message bodies are never requested.

Details of what is stored and for how long are in the privacy policy.

Why it exists

I was not measuring anything about my own life — not my sleep, not my weight, not where my hours went. This tool exists to count those things and put the numbers in front of me each morning, without interpreting them for me.
